## 3. Why IMU faults are not helped (analysis)

### 3.1 Oracle test: perfect reliability, current design

The same EKF was fed the **true** labels instead of the LSTM's predictions:

| | Fixed | Heuristic | LSTM | Oracle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IMU-fault wins vs fixed | — | 44/112 | 32/112 | 56/112 |
| IMU-fault mean RMSE (m) | 5.706 | 5.982 | 5.852 | 5.734 |
| Odom-fault wins vs fixed | — | 65/112 | **89/112** | 76/112 |

- **With the linear mapping, even perfect reliability does not improve IMU faults** (5.734 vs 5.706 m).
- **The LSTM beats the oracle on odom.** The reliability labels are therefore *not* the values that minimise RMSE. LSTM label accuracy and EKF improvement must be reported as separate claims.

### 3.2 Oracle mapping test (pre-registered variants, true labels)

| r_imu mapping | IMU wins | IMU mean RMSE | Rule (> 50% wins **and** RMSE < fixed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| linear R/(r+ε) | 56/112 | 5.734 | fail |
| squared R/r² | 57/112 | 5.675 | pass (marginal) |
| **gate at 0.5** | **73/112** | **4.861 (−15%)** | **pass** |
| ideal exclusion (r < 0.999) | 75/112 | 4.708 (−17%) | pass |

All variants equal fixed on the no-fault baselines. **With perfect reliability, hard gating works; soft scaling does not.**

### 3.3 Gate with the LSTM's own r_imu (pre-registered pass rule): **FAIL**

| | IMU wins | IMU mean RMSE | Odom wins | Baselines |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LSTM, linear | 32/112 | 5.852 | 89/112 | 1.1709 |
| LSTM, gate 0.5 | 38/112 | **6.343** | 93/112 | 1.1660 |

The rules were: IMU wins > 50% **FAIL**; IMU RMSE < fixed **FAIL**; baselines ≤ 1% worse **PASS**; odom not down > 5 **PASS**. **The gate is not adopted.**

### 3.4 LSTM r_imu as a fault detector (rows; descriptive only)

| IMU fault | Detected at 0.5 | False alarms at 0.5 | AUC | Mean r: faulty / clean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 46.1% | 3.0% | 0.801 | 0.558 / 0.862 |
| noise | **95.6%** | — | — | 0.241 / — |
| bias | **5.7%** | — | — | 0.819 / — |
| drift | 25.6% | 0.0% | 0.825 | 0.690 / 0.869 |
| dropout | 19.0% | 5.8% | 0.572 | 0.723 / 0.818 |
| delay | **0 faulty rows** | 0.8% | — | — / 0.898 |
| baselines | — | 4.9% | — | — / 0.823 |

### 3.5 Interpretation

1. **Noise is detected, but a single-row gate leaks.** About 4% of noise rows pass the gate at full trust, and with σ up to about 20 rad each one wrecks the heading. The gate is too brittle; detection is not the problem.
2. **Bias is unobservable with these sensors.** It is injected from row 0 (`start_frac = 0`). With tight R_imu the EKF simply adopts the offset heading, and its innovations look normal afterwards. A constant offset also leaves the turn *rate* unchanged, so `wheel_imu_disagreement` can't reveal it. Only an absolute heading reference could, and LiDAR is inert at ALPHA = 0.98. **This is a physical limit, not an LSTM failure.**
3. **Dropout (frozen heading) is weakly detected** (AUC 0.57). There is real room to improve.
4. **Label gap:** `generate_reliability_labels.py` labels IMU delay faults as r_imu = 1. No reliability-based method, the oracle included, can react to delay.

## 4. What the paper can and cannot claim

**Supported:**
- The LSTM estimates sensor reliability accurately and generalises across all 7 LOBO folds (0.255 / 0.316 vs a 0.343 / 0.386 fit-mean).
- LSTM-adaptive covariance clearly improves robustness to **odometry** faults (79% vs fixed; 58% for the χ² heuristic).
- **For IMU faults, covariance scaling is limited.** The oracle analysis isolates three causes: the mapping (soft scaling vs gating), detection quality per fault type, and the physical unobservability of a constant heading bias.
- Phase 1's single-step χ² test fails on persistent IMU faults (39%), consistent with the self-referential residual explanation.

**Not supported (do not claim):**
- That Phase 2 "fixes" Phase 1's IMU weakness (29% vs 39%).
- That LiDAR fusion improves accuracy at the deployed weight.
- **Any accuracy gain from the RNN stage, or from the RNN + LiDAR pipeline** (§10: it does not generalise; the §2.3 gains depend on the evaluated session's own ground-truth statistics).
- That the fault magnitudes are "realistic": IMU and odom severities are scaled to signal std (IMU yaw σ 1.5–23 rad; odom-noise RMSE up to about 5,000 m). Describe them as **stress-test magnitudes**.
- Any explanation of the saturated-session anomalies (§2.2): untested.

**Reporting caveats:**
- **Saturation correction:** it is derived from the model's limits but is **not an exact plant model** (the correlation of executed speed with GT speed is 0.19–0.41 on the saturated sessions).
- **Single seed:** results come from one run (seed 42). Mean ± std over 3–5 seeds is recommended for the RNN and LSTM.
- **Delay window trimming:** large delays shorten the shared time window (e.g. 100318 lost 37 rows at "large").
- **IMU noise ratios on clean baselines are very large**, because the baseline RMSE is small (0.33 m).

## 14. Outlier / spike check (2026-10-05). **Decision: no outlier filter**

- **Question:** should a proper (local) outlier filter replace the removed global IQR clipping?
- **Test 1** (`scripts/spike_diagnostic.py`, robust second-difference test): **invalid on this data.**
  - 64–80% of filtered odometry samples lie on exact straight segments, because odom is published below 50 Hz and linearly resampled.
  - Its robust noise scale was therefore about 0, and normal command changes were flagged as "spikes" of 10⁷–10⁸ × noise.
  - Its pre-stated "CONSIDER" verdict was **not acted on**, and is recorded as an instrument failure.
- **Test 2** (physical units; largest single-sample deviation from the neighbours' line, filtered data, all 10 sessions):

| Signal | Zero-curvature samples | Largest deviation | % of signal range | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| odom pos_x / pos_y | 64% | 0.006 m | 0.07–0.09% | EKF (s) |
| odom yaw | 80% | 0.018 rad | 0.19% | EKF (φ) |
| IMU yaw | 34% | 0.007 rad | 0.08% | EKF (θ) |
| IMU yaw rate | 0% | 0.149 rad/s | 2.9% | LSTM feature |
| odom linear_x | 75% | 0.154 m/s | 5.4% | LSTM feature |
| odom angular_z | 79% | 0.435 rad/s | 7.2% | LSTM feature |
| IMU acc_x | 0% | 50 m/s² | (contact impulses) | LSTM feature |

- **Interpretation:**
  - **Every signal the EKF uses is clean** (largest deviation ≤ 0.19% of its range).
  - **The odom velocity "deviations" are steps, not spikes.** The second-difference test returns half a step at a corner, and the values (0.15 m/s, 0.43 rad/s) match half of the teleop command steps. Filtering them would remove real dynamics.
  - **IMU acceleration jumps** are contact impulses. The signal is an LSTM input only.
- **Decision:** keep Savitzky–Golay only; no outlier filter.
- **Methods statement:** *global IQR clipping was removed because it discarded genuine motion; a local spike check found no isolated outliers in the signals used by the filter (largest deviation ≤ 0.2% of signal range).*

### 14.1 IMU acceleration events (`scripts/imu_acc_investigation.py`, read-only)
- **Count:** 10 sessions, 10.2 min, 30,568 raw IMU samples. **9 samples with |a| > 10 m/s² (0.029%)**, all single-sample. They are **6 distinct moments**; 3 of them appear on both the x and y axes.
- **Not preprocessing artifacts** (0 filtered-only events). **Not timing discontinuities** (no dt anomalies).

| Session, t (s) | IMU a (m/s²) | True acc (m/s²) | Context | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100318, 8.7 | x −1215, y −477 | **−22.4** | no command step | **Real abrupt stop (collision); IMU magnitude about 55× the true value** |
| 100318, 16.4 | x −71, y 11 | −3.3 | cmd step 1.5, vertical jolt 44 | Impact plus command step |
| 141740, 113.4 | x −18 | −1.1 | 0.32 m from a wall, vertical jolt 7.8 | Wall contact |
| 141740, 120.5 | x −16, y 15 | ≈0 | vertical jolt 10.6 | Chassis jolt |
| 095427, 29.2 | x −10.6 | −3.5 | cmd step 2.6 | Hard braking at a command step |
| 132243, 29.8 | x −15.1 | −3.6 | cmd step 0.8 | Hard braking at a command step |

- **Conclusion:** every event coincides with a **real physical event** in the simulation (a collision, a wall contact, a jolt or hard braking). The IMU reports it as a one-sample impulse **3–55× larger than the true acceleration**, a contact-solver artifact.
  - (The event labelled "isolated_glitch" is the y-component of the 100318 collision.)
  - None of these are random sensor glitches, and the EKF does not use IMU acceleration.
- **Decision:** no change to the current pipeline or results.
- **For the later IMU retrain (D6):** pre-register a physical clip of the IMU acceleration feature (e.g. ±25 m/s², above the largest true acceleration seen, 22 m/s²). It keeps each event's sign and timing but removes the unphysical magnitude.
- **Paper (Methods/limitations):** *"Simulated IMU acceleration contained rare single-sample impulses (9 samples, 0.03%) at collisions, wall contacts and step commands, 3–55× larger than the true acceleration; acceleration is used only as an LSTM input."*

**What this changes in the claims (important):**
